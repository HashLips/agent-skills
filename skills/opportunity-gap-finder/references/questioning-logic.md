# Questioning Logic

## Summary

- Branch from signals instead of using a fixed questionnaire.
- Push toward actors, observed behaviour, consequence, alternatives, contribution, and user fit.

## When the User Has No Starting Point

Ask about:

- people, places, communities, industries, or activities they can access,
- recurring frustrations, requests, transitions, exclusions, or wasted resources they have observed,
- skills, credibility, relationships, spaces, equipment, knowledge, or rights they can use,
- the kind of value and commitment they want,
- unacceptable domains, risks, or ways of working.

Select two or three search territories. Do not generate a long list of generic ideas.

## When the Scenario Is Vague

Clarify:

- the change
- affected actors
- location or market
- timeframe
- observed behaviour
- desired type of value
- user constraints and access

## When the User Proposes a Product Too Early

Ask:

- Who specifically needs this?
- What are they doing today?
- What evidence shows the current method is inadequate?
- What outcome matters to them?
- What would happen if they did nothing?
- What alternatives already exist, including informal ones?
- What evidence would make you abandon or reshape the idea?

## When the User Reports a Complaint

Ask:

- Who says this?
- How often?
- What do they do instead?
- What does it cost them?
- Have they attempted to solve it?
- Why did existing solutions fail?
- What contribution have they already made to solve it?

## When a Pain Sounds Weak

Investigate consequence, frequency, urgency, and willingness to act.

## When a Pain Is Severe but Rare

Assess whether the bounded impact alone meets the user's success threshold. If external support is required, test suitable contribution paths such as direct payment, sponsorship, grants, public provision, collective support, volunteering, partnership, or service to adjacent groups.

## When the Audience Is Large but Pain Is Weak

Look for a narrower high-intensity segment.

## When There Is No Obvious Buyer

Determine whether the pursuit is intrinsically valuable or whether a beneficiary, sponsor, institution, community, volunteer base, grant-maker, or public body can sustain it. Do not discard non-commercial value, but do not hide the sustaining problem.

## When the User Wants a Personal or Creative Project

Prioritise:

- intrinsic value and desired learning,
- audience or participant benefit where relevant,
- realistic scope and completion path,
- motivation and maintenance fit,
- a small version that produces value even without growth.

Do not impose venture-scale criteria.

## When the User Wants a Business

Investigate buyer urgency, budget, access, switching behaviour, delivery cost, repeatability, distribution, and why the value can be captured rather than only created.

## When the Solution Requires Education

Treat education and onboarding as part of the product, not as external marketing.

## When Trust Is Low

Investigate whether transparency, proof, open source, certification, guarantees, community, visible identity, or auditability can become part of the solution.

## When the Market Is Accelerating

Investigate:

- growing search and discussion
- new spending
- new roles
- repeated beginner questions
- poor standards
- fragmented tools
- high experimentation
- strong perceived upside

## When the Market May Be Decelerating

Investigate:

- falling engagement
- reduced funding or spending
- negative sentiment
- platform consolidation
- declining search interest
- users abandoning projects
- regulation
- weakening perceived return
- a shift from experimentation to efficiency

Do not assume acceleration is always better. Mature or declining markets can still contain valuable consolidation, migration, compliance, repair, and efficiency opportunities.

## When Evidence Conflicts

Segment by actor, context, location, timing, and behaviour. Preserve the contradiction until a credible explanation or targeted test resolves it.

## When Stakes Are High

For health, safety, legal, financial, environmental, vulnerable-population, or irreversible decisions:

- increase the evidence threshold,
- consult qualified or authoritative sources,
- prioritise consent, harm prevention, and reversibility,
- avoid recommendations that exceed the available expertise or evidence.
