# Opportunity Archetypes

## Summary

- Use these archetypes to broaden opportunity discovery across commercial and non-commercial domains.
- Match evidence to gap types before choosing a mechanism.

## 1. Access Gap

A valuable capability exists, but ordinary users cannot access it.

## 2. Expertise Gap

The process requires specialised knowledge.

## 3. Workflow Gap

The capability exists, but the steps are fragmented or manual.

## 4. Trust Gap

Users cannot verify providers, claims, data, or outcomes.

## 5. Coordination Gap

Multiple actors must cooperate, but incentives or communication fail.

## 6. Information Gap

People cannot find, understand, organise, or compare what they need.

## 7. Integration Gap

Tools exist but do not work together.

## 8. Deployment Gap

People can create something but cannot reliably launch, host, maintain, distribute, or operate it.

## 9. Measurement Gap

People cannot determine whether their actions are working.

## 10. Compliance Gap

Rules create work, uncertainty, or risk.

## 11. Identity Gap

People want to become a type of person or organisation but lack a credible path.

## 12. Safety Gap

A new behaviour creates physical, financial, psychological, legal, or reputational risk.

## 13. Attention Gap

A useful capability consumes too much time, focus, or cognitive energy.

## 14. Transition Gap

People are moving from an old system to a new one and need migration, translation, training, or compatibility.

## 15. Infrastructure Gap

Many participants repeatedly rebuild the same foundational component.

## 16. Affordability Gap

A valuable outcome is available, but the price, financing, ownership model, or hidden cost excludes important actors.

## 17. Availability and Distribution Gap

A suitable solution exists elsewhere but does not reach a location, language, channel, schedule, or population.

## 18. Experience Gap

The functional outcome exists, but dignity, ease, enjoyment, belonging, identity, or human experience is poor.

## 19. Representation Gap

People, places, needs, or perspectives are absent, distorted, or poorly served in media, research, design, or decision-making.

## 20. Maintenance and Repair Gap

Creation receives attention, but upkeep, repair, continuity, aftercare, or end-of-life handling is neglected.

## 21. Stewardship Gap

A shared resource has value, but ownership, governance, incentives, or long-term care is unclear.

## 22. Sustainability Gap

An intervention creates value but lacks durable funding, labour, participation, governance, or operating incentives.

## Usage Rule

Archetypes organise inquiry; they do not prove demand. An opportunity may combine several gap types, but name the primary gap and support it with actor-specific evidence.
