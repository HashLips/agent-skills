# Evidence

## Summary

- Use evidence appropriate to the claim, domain, geography, and decision.
- Triangulate important claims and report confidence separately from attractiveness.

## Evidence Ledger

Maintain a compact record for decision-relevant claims:

```text
Claim:
Claim type:
Source or observation:
Date and context:
Strength:
Consistency:
Limitations or bias:
Supports or contradicts:
Confidence:
```

Distinguish:

- **Sourced fact** — traceable to a named, relevant source.
- **User observation** — first-hand or reported experience with stated context.
- **Inference** — interpretation derived from available evidence.
- **Hypothesis** — plausible claim requiring evidence.
- **Unknown** — unresolved information that could change the decision.

Never invent statistics, quotations, customers, competitors, regulations, or source details. When live research is unavailable, say so and treat current-market claims as provisional.

## Evidence Strength

Judge evidence by relevance, recency, directness, independence, sample quality, and observed behaviour.

- **Strong** — repeated real behaviour, payment or commitment, operational records, representative research, or independent sources converging on the same claim.
- **Moderate** — repeated interviews, credible contextual data, persistent workarounds, or several relevant but imperfect sources.
- **Weak** — isolated anecdotes, self-reported intent, broad trend data, social engagement, or a source with unclear relevance.
- **Absent** — no evidence beyond intuition or analogy.

Evidence strength is claim-specific. A source proving that a population is growing does not prove that the population wants the proposed intervention.

Report consistency separately:

- **Aligned** — relevant evidence points in the same material direction.
- **Mixed** — results differ but may be explained by actor, context, timing, or method.
- **Contradictory** — credible evidence supports incompatible conclusions that remain unresolved.

Derive overall confidence from strength, consistency, and limitations. Strong contradictory evidence can produce low confidence in the conclusion while still being high-quality evidence.

## Evidence Routes

Choose sources based on the opportunity:

- Observe workflows, environments, transactions, queues, abandonment, and workarounds.
- Interview affected actors, buyers, operators, experts, detractors, and people who chose alternatives.
- Examine complaints, reviews, support questions, community discussions, procurement requests, and job postings.
- Compare alternatives, prices, availability, distribution, retention, failure history, and switching costs.
- Use credible demographic, regulatory, scientific, trade, financial, cultural, or local data.
- Test revealed commitment through time, money, data, referrals, access, behaviour change, or permission.

For sensitive, regulated, safety-critical, or high-stakes domains, prioritise qualified expertise and authoritative sources. Do not infer safety, legality, or clinical effectiveness from popularity.

## Triangulation

Before assigning high confidence to a central claim, seek:

1. one direct behavioural or operational signal,
2. one affected-actor perspective,
3. one independent contextual or market source,
4. evidence about existing alternatives or non-consumption,
5. at least one deliberate search for disconfirming evidence.

Use fewer sources only when the next action is cheap and reversible. Require stronger evidence as cost, irreversibility, safety risk, or reputational exposure increases.

## Demand and Value Signals

Do not collapse these into a single idea of "interest":

```text
Attention → complaint → stated intent → attempted workaround → commitment
→ adoption → repeated use → sustained value or impact
```

For commercial pursuits, contribution may include payment, signed intent, procurement effort, or budget reallocation.

For non-commercial pursuits, contribution may include attendance, volunteer time, access, participation, referrals, behaviour change, institutional support, or measurable benefit.

## Confidence Rules

- Report confidence for the problem, actor, alternative weakness, proposed mechanism, access path, and sustaining model.
- Lower confidence when evidence is stale, geographically mismatched, self-selected, vendor-produced, or based on stated intent.
- Record contradictions rather than averaging them away.
- State what evidence would materially raise, lower, or reverse the recommendation.
- Do not use decimal precision or precise success probabilities to disguise uncertainty.
