# Quality Checklist

## Summary

- Use this as the final quality gate before declaring a strong opportunity.
- Treat anti-patterns as hard stops.

## Quality Standard

A strong opportunity should usually demonstrate:

- A clear pursuit purpose and success threshold
- A specific actor
- A repeated behaviour
- A meaningful friction, blocked outcome, or underused capability
- A defensible root-cause explanation
- A measurable consequence
- Evidence of current alternatives, workarounds, or non-consumption
- A clear value mechanism
- A plausible adoption path
- A realistic access or distribution path
- A plausible sustaining model
- Fit with the user's resources, motivation, and boundaries
- Acceptable legal, ethical, safety, environmental, and social risk
- A testable risky assumption

## Anti-Patterns

Do not:

- Generate ten generic startup ideas immediately
- Use trend popularity as proof of demand
- Assume every problem needs an app
- Treat every inconvenience as a business
- Inflate scores to please the user
- Present invented market statistics
- Confuse a new capability with a validated need
- Treat an absent direct competitor as proof of an open opportunity
- Ignore indirect substitutes, doing nothing, failed attempts, or switching costs
- Recommend a full build before testing
- Ignore existing substitutes
- Ignore the cost of behaviour change
- Use a precise success probability without sufficient data
- Let pursuer enthusiasm replace affected-actor evidence
- Force commercial or technology criteria onto every pursuit
- Reject non-commercial value merely because no buyer exists
- Recommend growth when a bounded project better matches the user's goal
- Average away contradictory evidence
- Ignore harms, excluded actors, maintenance, or end-of-life consequences

## Delivery Checks

- [ ] Pursuit criteria and scope are explicit
- [ ] Investigation followed context → actors → behaviour → alternatives → gap → root cause → need → mechanism
- [ ] Sourced facts, user observations, inferences, hypotheses, contradictions, and unknowns are distinguished
- [ ] Major claims use relevant evidence and current claims are sourced or marked provisional
- [ ] Individual hard gates, comparative rank, evidence strength, consistency, and resulting confidence are reported separately
- [ ] Alternatives, access, sustainability, user fit, and kill factors were checked
- [ ] Lead opportunity was stress-tested, including harms and disconfirming evidence
- [ ] Validation tests the riskiest assumption at a proportionate evidence level
- [ ] Recommendation language matches uncertainty and does not imply guaranteed success
- [ ] Decision is one of the allowed outcomes, or the next evidence is specified
