# Cross-Domain Examples

## Summary

- These abbreviated examples demonstrate domain neutrality and calibrated conclusions.
- They illustrate reasoning shape, not reusable claims or completed market research.

## Local Service: Household Repair

### Starting Point

A user with practical repair skills wants a modest local income stream with low startup cost.

### Early Investigation

- **Actor** — renters and older homeowners who postpone small repairs.
- **Observed behaviour to verify** — bundling tasks, asking neighbours, living with faults, or hiring services with high minimum charges.
- **Possible gap** — small jobs may be too minor for established contractors but too risky or difficult for residents.
- **Alternatives** — do-it-yourself work, family help, general contractors, property managers, or doing nothing.
- **Opportunity mechanism** — a trusted, fixed-scope neighbourhood repair round rather than an app.
- **Critical unknowns** — job frequency, travel density, liability, trust, pricing, and whether enough residents commit.
- **Decision** — validate before committing through interviews, a limited service day, and clear safety boundaries.

## Personal Project: Community Oral History

### Starting Point

A user wants a meaningful creative project using interviewing and audio-production skills. Income is optional; completion and community value matter more.

### Early Investigation

- **Actors** — long-term residents, families, schools, libraries, and local cultural groups.
- **Desired progress** — preserve lived histories and make them accessible without placing a heavy burden on contributors.
- **Possible gap** — stories may be valued but remain undocumented because recording, consent, curation, and preservation require coordination.
- **Alternatives** — family archives, social media posts, formal museum projects, or no documentation.
- **Opportunity mechanism** — a small, consent-led seasonal collection with a library partner.
- **Critical unknowns** — contributor interest, representation, permissions, archive ownership, accessibility, and long-term stewardship.
- **Decision** — pursue a small version if a partner and several contributors commit; do not score it by venture-market size.

## Public-Interest Initiative: Rural Appointment Access

### Starting Point

A community group reports that residents miss important appointments because travel is unreliable.

### Early Investigation

- **Actors** — residents without reliable transport, caregivers, clinics, transport providers, and local authorities.
- **Consequences** — potentially severe, but they must be supported by local operational or health-service evidence.
- **Alternatives** — family lifts, taxis, public transport, remote appointments, rescheduling, or missed care.
- **Possible root causes** — route gaps, scheduling mismatch, affordability, accessibility, coordination, or information failure.
- **Distinct mechanisms** — scheduling coordination, volunteer transport, route redesign, travel vouchers, mobile services, or remote-access support.
- **Critical unknowns** — safety, safeguarding, insurance, demand patterns, authority, funding, and whether transport is the true cause.
- **Decision** — investigate now, but require authoritative and expert evidence before choosing or piloting a mechanism.

## Technology Market: AI-Assisted Work

### Starting Point

A user asks for the largest opportunity in an attention-heavy technology market.

### Early Investigation

- **Do not infer** — market attention does not prove a specific unmet need or willingness to switch.
- **Segment** — distinguish professions, expertise levels, workflows, buyers, risk levels, and existing tools.
- **Observe** — repeated verification, lost context, manual transfer, compliance work, abandoned use, and spending.
- **Compare** — built-in platform features, established products, internal processes, consultants, and non-use.
- **Generate later** — only propose software, training, services, standards, or governance after an actor-specific gap is supported.
- **Decision** — a rapid scan may identify search territories, but no "biggest gap" is defensible without current evidence and a defined user success criterion.
