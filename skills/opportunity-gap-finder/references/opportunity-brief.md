# Opportunity Brief

## Summary

- Deliver a decision-ready brief matched to the user's pursuit criteria.
- Complete only when the next commitment is justified or the missing evidence is explicit.

## Opening Prompt

When activated with no starting point, ask:

```text
What would make an opportunity worth pursuing for you?

Share any domain, place, group, problem, change, skill, asset, or early idea you want
to explore, plus constraints such as time, budget, location, risk, or desired impact.
If you have no starting point, I can help narrow promising search territories first.
```

If the user already supplied enough context, do not repeat these questions. Summarise the starting point, state what remains assumed, and ask the first 3–6 high-value investigation questions.

## Final Opportunity Brief

The final output should contain:

### 1. Pursuit Criteria and Scope

The user's purpose, success threshold, relevant assets, constraints, boundaries, and search territory.

### 2. Context and Actors

The relevant conditions or changes, highest-value affected actors, desired progress, and who benefits, decides, contributes, or blocks adoption.

### 3. Current Behaviour and Alternatives

What actors do today, including substitutes, workarounds, non-consumption, failed prior attempts, and switching barriers.

### 4. Ranked Gaps

Ranked frictions, blocked outcomes, or underused capabilities. For each, distinguish sourced facts, user observations, inferences, hypotheses, contradictions, and unknowns.

### 5. Root Causes and Need Statements

The likely causes and clear outcome-focused needs without prescribing a solution.

### 6. Opportunity Hypotheses

Usually three to five, unless the evidence strongly supports fewer.

For each:

- **Actor and need** — who needs what progress and why it matters.
- **Opportunity mechanism** — how value would be created.
- **Value proposition** — why this is meaningfully better than alternatives.
- **Value exchange** — what sustains the effort: money, time, permission, participation, sponsorship, or another contribution.
- **User fit** — why this user could credibly pursue it.
- **Hard gates and comparative rank** — pass, uncertainty, failure, and trade-offs.
- **Evidence confidence** — strength and limitations by major claim.
- **Main risks** — including harm, adoption, access, delivery, and sustainability.

### 7. Recommended Lead

State why it ranks first for this user, what must be true, and why the recommendation is proportionate to the evidence.

### 8. Validation Plan

Define the smallest safe experiments, thresholds, boundaries, and decisions. Do not default to building.

### 9. Unknowns and Disconfirming Evidence

State contradictions, missing evidence, evidence against the lead, and what would reverse the recommendation.

### 10. Decision

Use one of:

- Investigate now
- Validate before committing
- Pursue a small version
- Monitor
- Reframe
- Reject for now

## Completion Rule

The skill is complete only when it has either:

1. identified at least one sufficiently supported opportunity and a proportionate validation or pursuit plan, or
2. concluded that the evidence does not justify pursuit and specified exactly what must be learned next.

Do not force an opportunity where none is supported.
